Sector rotation — Relative Rotation Graph

The 11 SPDR sector ETFs plotted against SPY. Sectors are listed below the chart in hunting order: Stage 2 first, then Stage 1, then Stage 3, then Stage 4 — and within each stage, by relative momentum.

Stage comes from each ETF's own weekly chart. The plot position comes from its performance relative to SPY. Those are different questions, and a sector can look good on one and bad on the other.

What the two axes mean

x — JdK RS-Ratio (relative trend). Where the sector is versus SPY. Above 100 means outperforming. It is heavily smoothed (10-week average of the price relative, then normalised over 40 weeks), so it is stable and slow. By construction it crosses 100 late: the sector has already been outperforming for weeks by the time the line moves through the axis.

y — JdK RS-Momentum (rate of change of the x-axis). Where the sector is going. It is the 4-week rate of change of RS-Ratio, normalised the same way, and it is deliberately left unsmoothed — so it turns early and it crosses 100 often. Early and noisy are the same property.

The two axes are not independent. The y-axis is computed from the x-axis. You are looking at the level and the slope of one quantity, not at two separate measurements. Practically: x is what you get paid on, y is what tells you when.

How to read the plot

Quadrants. Axes cross at 100. leading outperforming and still accelerating · weakening still ahead but losing steam · lagging behind and still falling back · improving behind but turning up.

Rotation is clockwise: leading → weakening → lagging → improving → leading. A sector moving anticlockwise is not rotating, it is stalling.

Tails are the last 12 weeks, oldest thin and faint, newest thick and solid. Tail colour is the quadrant the tail ends in; tail thickness is distance from the 100/100 crosshair.

Read direction, not position. A sector sitting in improving and travelling up-and-right is converting to leadership. The same sector travelling up-and-left is drifting further from the benchmark and, under clockwise rotation, will most likely loop back into lagging without ever converting.

For Stage 1→2 hunting, the highest-value event is RS-Ratio crossing above 100 — the improving→leading conversion, flagged in the Cross column.

Two things this plot cannot tell you.

1. Everything here is relative. A sector can sit in improving simply because it is falling more slowly than SPY. That is why the Stage column exists and why the table is ordered by it rather than by quadrant.

2. Each sector is normalised against its own 40-week history, so a low-volatility sector shows large RS deviations for small relative moves. Quadrant membership and direction of travel are comparable across sectors; the decimal values are not.

How the stage is calculated

One function — classify() in src/stages.py — used by the sector monitor, the Weinstein strategy and every chart in the framework. It reads the security's own weekly closes; no relative strength is involved.

  • MA30, the 30-week average, is the decision line. MA10, the 10-week average — which is the 50-day average — is the warning line.
  • Transitions fire on the week their trigger is true. There is no confirmation delay: the break of the 30-week MA is the signal, and a label that arrives weeks later is worse than useless.
1 → 2close above MA30 and MA30 rising
1 → 4close below MA30 and MA30 falling
2 → 4close below MA30
2 → 3close below MA10
3 → 4close below MA30
3 → 2close back above MA10 and MA30 rising
4 → 1close back above MA10 and MA30 not falling

The fast line governs the warnings (2→3, 4→1); the slow line governs the commitments (3→4, 1→2, 2→4). Stage 3 means the advance is not confirmed right now, not "topping" — so it is common, transient, and 3→2 is a normal event.

The ±0.5% band, the 3-week confirmation and the 15% prior-decline threshold are calibration choices, not Weinstein's numbers.
